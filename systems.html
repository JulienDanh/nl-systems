Preflop Primer

Post-flop outputs are a direct consequence of preflop inputs. Matching these ranges makes solver study relevant.

Core idea: all RFI ranges are linear

You open the best hands first. Ace-high is most concentrated, then king-high, etc. Ranges widen as you move later in position.

Stack-depth sensitivity: shorter → slightly tighter, but backbones are stable. Memorize the perimeter, not every combo.

System 1 — UTG RFI vs BB Call · C-betting

UTG opens, BB calls, BB checks. We decide our flop c-bet.

Two flop buckets

BucketBoardsDefault
1 · T-high+T, J, Q, K, A highC-bet 100% Subject to risk factors.
2 · 9-high & below9-high to trips-deuces~70/30 bet/check Strong+weak bet; medium checks.
Bucket 1 occurs far more often — one ace makes a flop ace-high. Highest-ROI piece.

Why bet 100% on T-high+?

Overpair asymmetry: UTG has far more strong pairs than BB caller. Shorter stacks amplify → bet more. Deeper → more caution.

Bet MORE when shallow, not less. Most players do the opposite — correct the leak.

Sizing

Start at 1/4 to 1/3 pot. Solver examples land at 25–40%.

Risk Factors (override "c-bet 100%")

1. Straights possible primary

  • 1 straight → still bet frequently.
  • 3 straights → slow down heavily.

2. Ace-high monotone secondary

Bet very strong (flush, sets) + very weak (trash); check medium (88 no-heart, weak aces, KK no-heart).

3. AKx family secondary

AK2, AK3, AK4 — "looks like that." Slow down.

4. Stack depth secondary

Deeper (→150bb) → caution. Shallower (→20bb) → lean into 100%.

5. High-Low-Low (paired low card) secondary

High card (T–A) with two paired low cards: A22, K33, Q33, J66, T55.

  • Bet: trips, overpairs (JJ, TT), very weak.
  • Check: underpairs (99–66), medium aces, AK/AQ/AT.
  • Blocker nuance: ATo bets more when ace blocks backdoor FD CRs.
Not High-High-Low. KK3 rainbow is not a risk factor — c-bet 100%. Only paired low under high counts.

Risk Factors

Ace-high boards

  • Stack depth: deeper → more checking. Shallow → bet 100%.
  • Monotone: substantially more checking.
  • Paired: more checking (A♠ J♥ J♦ · paired → check KK/QQ/medium; bet Jx + trash).

T–K high boards (with deuce/3)

  • Two low cards: biggest risk factor (K♠ 6♥ 3♥ · two low cards, K35 — the second low card). K92, KT3 are fine.
  • Monotone: substantially more checking.
  • Paired: less concerning here. Stack depth: noisier.

The Pyramid (when you must check)

On boards where BTN misses (no 8+, low interaction):

Bet the top (sets, overpairs, top pair) + bet the bottom (trash bluffs, selectively — can't bet all bluffs) + check the middle (underpairs, ace-high, KQ, middle pairs).

Two-suit awareness (key skill)

On two-tone boards, BB defends around two suits. Most only think about the flush-draw suit. Also block the second suit (non-flush-draw suit BB calls with via backdoor draws).

K83 two-tone (hearts+diamonds): KJ with heart+diamond = pure bet. KJ with spades+clubs (both off) = pure check. KJ with a heart blocks BB's heart-based continuing range with no equity risk.

System 3 — BB vs SB Limp Stab · Defending Flops

SB limps, BB checks, SB stabs. We defend from BB. Highest-impact scenario — BB win rate determines winner/loser.

Why BB matters: Skilled player goes from −112 bb/100 (walk-away) to −20 — 92 points of opportunity. UTG only has 27. BB skill is definitively most important.

The System

Step 1 — Fold target

SB bets 1bb into ~3.7bb. Risk/reward = 1/4 ≈ 25% fold. Defend ~75%. Focus on the 25% you fold.

Step 2 — Strategically relevant cards

Ace and Deuce are NOT strategically relevant. Everything has overcards to deuce / undercards to ace. No over-unders to either. Ignore them. Build strategy around other board cards.

Step 3 — Three defending mechanisms

MechanismExample (K77)
High-card defendingAll A-high pure, Q-high ~pure, J-high starts folding
Three to a straight98o, 86o, 65o — pure calls
Backdoor flush draw85 with spade pure; 85 no spade folds. High card of suit > low card.

Step 4 — Classify around the key card

CategoryDefinitionPlayability

Over-underOne over, one underSensitive — worst hands. BDFD often needed.
Double undersBoth belowOften fold — unless gut shots / 3-straight

Key Skills & Exceptions

Identify the worst hand on the board

On A42 → worst contain a 7 (97, T7). On K62 → worst are over-unders to 6 (73, 83). On KQ8 → worst are double-unders to 8 (65, 54, 53).

BDFD is not always enough. If you have one of the worst hands, even BDFD won't save it. 42 with spade on K77 still folds. Identify worst first, then check if BDFD rescues.

Heuristic: key card height

  • High key card (8): double-overs rare/valuable → rarely fold.
  • Low key card (5): double-overs common → fold more (plenty below).

Paired boards

Paired card unusable (can't have equity vs trips). Evaluate around unpaired high card. High-card defending dominates.

Preflop asymmetry

SB has a folding range; BB does not. 2x/3x favor BB. BB checking = capped (no AK/AQ/overpairs). SB has advantage on Broadway boards; BB on low boards.

System 4 — Missed River Bluffs

Two-system approach. Prioritize System 1 (easier) while acknowledging System 2 (solvers use it more).

System 1 — Bottom of range up

Bluff with weakest hands first — lowest EV when checking, lowest opportunity cost. Heuristic: prioritize the lowest card in the hand. 72o before 65o. 92o before 87o.

Low cards have good blocking effects vs linear ranges (deuce blocks few value, unblocks folds — opponents folded 2x preflop).

System 2 — Blocking effects

Bluff with combos that block opponent's calls/raises and unblock their folds, while checking opportunity cost. Very difficult to manage all three — System 1 is default.

Key blocking patterns

  • 3-flush river: bluff with one card of the flush suit. Blocks flushes AND hero calls.
  • Two-tone flop called: opponent called with 2 suits. Bluff avoiding those; prefer others.
  • High card vs low card of suit: high card heart is worse (blocks more folds). Low card heart less damaging.
  • Busted straight + busted flush draw: terrible bluff — blocks both folding regions.

The realism constraint

You can't always have the ideal bluff. If hearts bet flop+turn, you won't have hearts left on river. Don't wait for the perfect blocker — you'll have no bluffing range. Bluff with what you realistically arrived with.

Value bets require bluffs

If you identify value bets, you must have bluffs (in common scenarios). Bluffs need value to carry them.

System 6 — Check-Raising Top Pair (Short Stacks)

BB defender CR top pair on flop. Focus on short stacks (≤35bb).

Inflection: 35bb. Above → nuts-oriented CR (sets, two pair, TPTK mix). At/below → aggressive top-pair CR. Shorter = more CR.

The pattern

Top pair = CR; two pair/sets = trap (check-call) at short stacks. SPR short enough to shove river without raising.

System 7 — C-bet Folding Flops (vs Check-Raises)

You c-bet the flop as preflop raiser and face a CR. Which hands defend vs fold?

MDF calculation

Fold% = Risk / (Pot + Risk) — makes zero-equity bluffs indifferent. But villain's bluffs have real equity → actual fold% is LOWER. Defend more than MDF.

Example: raise to 4 into pot of 10.56 → 38% MDF → actual ~18–25% fold.

System 8 — Bet Sizing In Position

Sizing decisions for IP player on turn/river. Core mistake: betting too small.

IP advantage

  • IP checks → range uncaps on next card (turn improves hands).
  • OOP checks → IP punishes immediately.
  • IP can polarize more → IP bets larger on average.

Geometric betting

To get stack in over 2 streets: ~pot on both (equal fractions). E.g. 50bb: ~11bb turn → ~34bb river shove.

System 10 — River Value Betting

Central thesis: absolute strength is irrelevant. Relative strength (vs opponent's range) determines value.

Inflection points weakening villain's range

  • Villain checks turn or river → subtracts strong hands
  • No flop or turn raising → subtracts sets/2-pair/flushes
  • Very small bet sizes → thin value, not nuts
When villain's range weakens, your medium-strength hands become the relative nuts → bet for value.

Geometric sizing

Bet similar fractions each street (pot-pot-pot) to get stacks in with strong hands.

Key principles

Checking converts strong hands into bluff catchers. IP auto-polarizes — bets hands that beat you, checks hands you beat. You lose value against the entire medium-strength region.

Risk factors

  • River cards that reintroduce strength: fills straights/flushes → re-uncaps villain → smaller sizing.
  • Multi-way: IP checking back flop in multi-way = extremely capped (no sets, no TPTK). Largely ignore them.
  • Donk bets: strip from solver — most players miss them in metagame.

Core principle

Opponent's required bluff frequency = your pot odds. Need 25% → they must bluff 25% of betting range. If balanced → worst callable = worth 0. But better bluff catchers (block value / unblock bluffs) = worth significant EV.

Preflop awareness: BTN opens suited 5+ and offsuit 8+. 4-x/3-x unblock bluffs (not opened); 8-x/9-x block bluffs (opened).

Exceptions

  • Recreational players may bluff with too-strong hands → higher kickers marginally better.
  • Check-raise opportunity: if opponent bets too small IP (1/3 pot, thin value), CR second pair can fold better + call worse.
  • No natural bluffs: if no draws missed, opponent won't convert Ax to bluff → fold even if MDF says call.

CR sizing

Modest CR (to ~25bb) can get hands you beat to call (KQ, QJ) and hands that beat you to fold (A-3/4/5♥).

Common mistakes

  2. Over-defending weak pairs/draws on A/K-high — pocket 7s/8s/9s, BDFDs are all folds.

  • vs jam on low disconnected board: often a call (neither range hits).

Cross-System Principles

The recurring themes across all 12 systems. Internalize these and most decisions follow.

#Principle
1Know preflop ranges (memorize perimeters) — foundation for all postflop decisions
2Relative > absolute hand strength — opponent's range determines your hand's value
3Inflection points — villain checks, small bets, lack of raising = range weakness
4Block value, unblock bluffs — blocker effects differentiate similar-looking hands
5Bet sizing sensitivity — small bets = wide defense; large bets = tight defense
6Board texture buckets — high vs low; paired/disconnected; rainbow vs two-tone
7Can't CR but called by worse → bet for value
8Don't let IP auto-polarize by checking when you have medium-strength hands
Use a small number of repeatable principles applied systematically rather than memorizing solver outputs. Use solvers to stress test your hypotheses, not to copy answers.

The pyramid (appears everywhere)

BET — very strong (value)
CHECK — medium strength
BET — very weak (bluff, selective)

This "bet top, bet bottom, check middle" structure recurs in Systems 2, 3, 4, 5, and beyond. The medium-strength hands are the most over-played category in the game.
