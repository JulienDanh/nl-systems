System 4 — Missed River Bluffs

Two-system approach. Prioritize System 1 (easier) while acknowledging System 2 (solvers use it more).

System 1 — Bottom of range up

Bluff with weakest hands first — lowest EV when checking, lowest opportunity cost. Heuristic: prioritize the lowest card in the hand. 72o before 65o. 92o before 87o.

Low cards have good blocking effects vs linear ranges (deuce blocks few value, unblocks folds — opponents folded 2x preflop).

System 2 — Blocking effects

Bluff with combos that block opponent's calls/raises and unblock their folds, while checking opportunity cost. Very difficult to manage all three — System 1 is default.

Key blocking patterns

  • 3-flush river: bluff with one card of the flush suit. Blocks flushes AND hero calls.
  • Two-tone flop called: opponent called with 2 suits. Bluff avoiding those; prefer others.
  • High card vs low card of suit: high card heart is worse (blocks more folds). Low card heart less damaging.
  • Busted straight + busted flush draw: terrible bluff — blocks both folding regions.

The realism constraint

You can't always have the ideal bluff. If hearts bet flop+turn, you won't have hearts left on river. Don't wait for the perfect blocker — you'll have no bluffing range. Bluff with what you realistically arrived with.

Value bets require bluffs

If you identify value bets, you must have bluffs (in common scenarios). Bluffs need value to carry them.

9-high · BvB · 95o
System agrees

System: Ideal bluffs = 4x (84,94,T4) but they had gutshots on turn → already barreled. 95o is realistically at the bottom. Pure bluff.

Solver: 95o pure bluff. 84/94 were turn barrels (gutshots) → won't reach river. Checking 95o = no bluffing range.
